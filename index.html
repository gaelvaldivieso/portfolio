Maintenance & Purchasing Portal · mid-size industrial printer, Ecuador

1/4Problem6 handoffs, 4 people, 3 channels, 2–3 days to an approved quote.

The client makes boxes and does binding, offset printing and digital finishing at its main plant and a site in the capital. It runs on FileMaker. When a machine broke and needed a spare part, the request went through 6 manual handoffs across 4 people and 3 channels (WhatsApp, verbal, email). From the first photo to an approved quote in purchasing typically took 2–3 days, sometimes a week (as reported by the team). Then the coordinator role became vacant.

The records had a gap too: breakdowns weren't logged until after repair.

2/4What I builtA PWA on top of FileMaker. Five steps, AI in four specific jobs, people still decide.

The maintenance module of an internal operations portal: an installable web app (PWA) that runs on top of FileMaker without replacing it.

  1. Report. Pick the machine, add up to 6 photos, write or dictate. AI suggests type and priority from the photo. The request lands in FileMaker the moment it is reported, with push and email alerts.
  2. Quote. Quotes arrive by email or upload. AI extracts vendor, amount and line items, converts to USD and flags duplicates.
  3. Approve. General management approves, approves with a condition, or rejects in one tap.
  4. Order. On approval, the purchase order is created automatically in FileMaker, with a PDF and an alert to purchasing.
  5. Close. The technician dictates notes and adds before/after photos. AI drafts the report, it is emailed, and FileMaker is updated.

Plus a dashboard with KPIs and failure frequency per machine, push notifications, device revocation, 2FA for admins and per-module permissions. The automatic purchase order was requested by general management mid-build.

3/4ResultCoordination survives a vacant role. 45–87 h/yr saved, payback under 3 months (est.).

The coordinator role was not refilled; the system is designed so coordination keeps working without it. At 7–10 breakdowns a month, that is an estimated 45–87 hours a year saved and payback in under 3 months (estimate). AI cost is estimated under 1 US cent per cycle. Early client feedback on time saved has been very positive (no formal testimonial yet).

In pilot with 3 active users. The quote → approve → purchase-order stage is built and tested with test orders; the first real end-to-end purchase is the next milestone.

4/4Lesson 01Brakes before accelerator.
Brakes before accelerator. When an automation is about to move real money, build the safety gates first.

The purchase order writes records in the client's system, so test mode, a per-quote lock against double-clicks, retries that resume without duplicating, and a manual-review panel all came before the feature itself. When something is unclear (VAT, an unknown vendor, line items that don't match), it stops and asks a person.

Architecture
  • Next.js 16 + React 19 PWA, serverless backend with 24 module endpoints, 3 screens.
  • AI does four jobs: photo classification (LLM vision), speech-to-text, quote extraction and report drafting, with structured outputs. People still create, approve and close.
  • FileMaker Data API: requests and machine catalogue in one database; purchase orders written as direct records across 9 layouts, no scripts. FileMaker stays the source of truth.
  • Managed auth/database layer, transactional email, Web Push, PDF generation.
What was hard
  • Integrate, don't replace. Orders are written as direct records from code, not opaque scripts.
  • Access is part of the project. The client's system sits behind restricted network access, so every production touch was planned and approved.
  • Measure the baseline carefully. In nearly two years of records (135 of 166 closed breakdowns), 81% looked like same-day closures, only because they were logged after the fix. Not a real speed metric.
  • Co-design with the client. The automatic purchase order came from general management during the build.
Next
  • Complete the first real end-to-end purchase.
  • Instrument the missing metrics: request source, voice and photo use, AI corrections, time per stage.
  • Preventive maintenance from each machine's history; more portal modules (Sales in use, production scheduler proposed).

Control Tower · card-delivery logistics operator, Ecuador

1/4ProblemNo live view of stuck shipments; someone scanned every point's Excel by hand.

The operation delivering bank cards had no real-time visibility into which shipments were stuck. Someone had to open every delivery point's daily Excel by hand and scan colour-coded pivot sheets for trouble.

2/4What I builtAn operational panel that answers live: healthy today? What needs follow-up? Which points are worst?

A full operational web panel (not an Excel sheet or a no-code dashboard) that answers, live: is the operation healthy today? Which shipments need follow-up right now? Which points are worst? A shipment is flagged at risk automatically when it goes 2+ days with no status change, or never left for delivery.

3/4ResultDaily manual review gone. Access segmented by Row Level Security. Deployed to operations.

Replaces the daily manual Excel review per delivery point. Access is segmented by security (Row Level Security) instead of emailing the whole sheet to everyone. Deployed to the operations team.

4/4Lesson 02The dashboard alone doesn't sell.
The dashboard alone doesn't sell. The value is the automation underneath it.

Delivery Assistant · digital field instructions

1/4ProblemCouriers had to memorise different documents, signatures and warnings per bank.

Couriers delivering cards for four banking clients (three banks and a credit union) had to memorise a long physical manual with different rules per bank: which documents to request, which signatures, which critical warnings.

2/4What I builtA step-by-step assistant in one HTML file: 1–3 questions, then the exact checklist.

A step-by-step assistant, built as one self-contained HTML/CSS/JS file with no external libraries, that guides the courier through 1–3 simple questions and produces the exact checklist: documents, required signatures, warnings (e.g. "deliver to the account holder only"), and what to upload to internal systems. All logic came from the company's real manual, nothing invented.

3/4ResultAll 18 rule combinations validated; ready for rollout across the 4 accounts.

Validated against all 18 possible rule combinations and ready for rollout to couriers across the 4 banking accounts. Designed and validated; rollout pending.

4/4Lesson 03Never invent. Say "verify in manual".
Never invent. Where the source is ambiguous, the tool says "verify in manual" instead of guessing.

A confident wrong answer at a customer's door costs more than a pause. The same rule applies to every AI output I ship: when the input is unclear, the system stops and hands the decision to a person.

Operations Bottleneck Diagnosis · mid-size industrial printer, Ecuador

1/4ProblemOne machine failing recurrently, unreported, with outsized impact on the chain.

One machine had been failing recurrently without the operator reporting it: a small-looking bottleneck with disproportionate impact across the whole chain.

2/4MethodMapped the whole process, sales to shipped product, with Theory of Constraints.

I mapped the entire process from sales to finished product leaving the factory, using a Theory of Constraints approach under my manager's guidance. That is how the small bottleneck's true, much larger impact became visible. Recommendation: invest in a second machine.

3/4ResultEst. 20% cost reduction and 24 h faster delivery if implemented.

Estimated 20% cost reduction and 24-hour faster delivery if the recommendation is implemented.

This engagement was operations and process consulting, not AI automation. Different discipline, same instinct.

4/4Lesson 05Find the constraint, quantify it, fix it.
Find the constraint, quantify it, fix it. Automation only pays when it hits the bottleneck.

Three questions before any build: how often is the task done, who does it today, and what happens when the information arrives late. If the answers don't point at a constraint, I don't build.
